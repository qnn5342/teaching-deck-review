# The1ight brand guidelines for teaching decks

This is a compact deck-production reference, not a replacement for the full brand system. Use it as a constraint layer after content and learning design are stable.

## Voice

- Vietnamese first; English for technical terms.
- Direct, sharp, warm, and practical.
- Pirate Captain + empathetic mentor: rebellious and street-smart without hype.
- Teach a skill or unlock a mindset block in every learner-facing section.
- Avoid generic AI promises, corporate training language, hollow inspiration, and aggressive sales copy.

## Visual direction

- Prefer clean, high-contrast layouts with one clear focal point.
- Use short headlines and enough empty space for live teaching.
- Screenshots should have a purpose: point to the interface area learners must inspect or act on.
- Do not add decorative icons, emojis, or badges merely to fill space.
- Keep question and reveal slides visually distinguishable when the class should answer first.
- Use the supplied template or approved brand palette. Do not invent exact brand colors from a screenshot.

## Logo rule

- Logo placement is optional unless the supplied template requires it.
- When a logo asset is supplied, default placement is the top-left corner with consistent safe-area padding and a size that does not compete with the headline.
- Use the approved logo variant for the background; do not recolor, stretch, crop, or recreate the logo.
- Keep the logo out of dense screenshot or activity areas.
- If no approved logo asset/template is supplied, mark logo placement as unverified and do not fabricate one.

## Required inputs for deterministic visual output

- Approved template or palette file.
- Approved logo asset, preferably an SVG or transparent PNG.
- Background mode: light/dark or per-slide rules.
- Preferred font(s), if they are not embedded in the template.
- Any footer or slide-number convention.

## Visual QA

After applying brand rules, render the deck and check logo placement, contrast, safe area, headline hierarchy, screenshot legibility, and text overflow. A logo being present is not evidence that the deck is on-brand.
