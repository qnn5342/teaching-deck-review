# Teaching style benchmark

Distill these traits from the approved benchmark deck; do not copy its unverified technical claims.

## Keep

- Start with a real learner question, previous homework, or classroom situation.
- Use action-oriented outcomes: what the learner can do after the lesson.
- Show concrete interfaces, files, screenshots, or artifacts when they clarify the concept.
- Separate question slides from reveal slides when the class should think first.
- Name practice explicitly and include time, checkpoints, and a debrief.
- Keep teacher operations visible when they help the class participate: chat, mic, reactions, office hours, or feedback.
- Use Vietnamese as the default and English for technical terms.
- Prefer direct, warm, practical language over corporate education copy.

## Fix

- Replace topic-only headlines with the point or learner action, but do not force every slide into a slogan.
- Preserve useful humor and classroom voice; humanizer is not a license to sterilize the deck.
- Keep evidence and caveats when a claim depends on provider, model, version, or runtime.
- Do not call a screenshot an activity unless learners have a task, output, success check, and debrief.
- Keep intentional question/reveal pairs; remove or merge only when the presentation purpose is absent.

## Anti-slop checks

- Cut generic promises, inflated conclusions, and repeated headings.
- Prefer a concrete subject and verb.
- Keep one cognitive job per slide where possible.
- Do not use density reduction as an excuse to remove necessary conditions.
- Do not invent learner stories, metrics, tool behavior, or case studies.
